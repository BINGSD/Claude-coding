Mechanics

Start every mechanics question with a clear diagram showing all the forces, then choose a positive direction. Use g = 9.8 m s−2 unless told otherwise, and round answers that use it to 2 or 3 significant figures.

Kinematics graphs & modelling

Year 1
Notes
  • Modelling terms: particle (size ignored), light (mass ignored), inextensible (string doesn't stretch), smooth (no friction), rigid (doesn't bend), uniform (mass evenly spread).
  • Displacement–time graph: gradient = velocity.
  • Velocity–time graph: gradient = acceleration; area under the graph = displacement.
  • Distance and speed are scalars. Displacement, velocity and acceleration are vectors, so they can be negative.
Key formulas · SI units
  • Displacementmetres (m)
  • Velocitym s−1
  • Accelerationm s−2
  • Forcenewtons (N) = kg m s−2
Worked exampleA car accelerates uniformly from rest to 15 m s⁻¹ in 5 s, travels at 15 m s⁻¹ for 20 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 10 s. Find the total distance travelled.
Show solutionHide solution
  1. Sketch the velocity–time graph: a trapezium with parallel sides 20 s (top) and 35 s (bottom), height 15.
  2. Distance = area = ½(20 + 35) × 15.
  3. Check by parts: 37.5 + 300 + 75 = 412.5.
412.5 m
Practice
  1. For the car in the worked example, find the deceleration in the final stage. [1 mark]
    Show answer

    1.5 m s⁻²

    Gradient = 15 ÷ 10.

  2. What does modelling a ball as a particle ignore? [1 mark]
    Show answer

    Its size (and so air resistance and rotation)

Watch out!

If the velocity goes negative, area below the time axis is displacement in the opposite direction. For distance, add the areas as positive values.

Constant acceleration (SUVAT)

Year 1
Notes
  • Only use SUVAT when acceleration is constant.
  • List the five quantities, fill in the three you know, and pick the equation that has the one you want and leaves out the one you don't.
  • For vertical motion under gravity, a = −g if you take up as positive. At maximum height, v = 0.
Key formulas
  • No sv = u + at
  • No vs = ut + ½at²
  • No us = vt − ½at²
  • No tv² = u² + 2as
  • No as = ½(u + v)t
Worked exampleA ball is thrown vertically upwards at 14.7 m s⁻¹. Find its maximum height and the time it takes to reach it.
Show solutionHide solution
  1. Up is positive: u = 14.7, v = 0, a = −9.8.
  2. Height: v² = u² + 2as gives 0 = 216.09 − 19.6s, so s = 11.025.
  3. Time: v = u + at gives 0 = 14.7 − 9.8t, so t = 1.5.
Maximum height 11.0 m after 1.5 s
Practice
  1. A stone is dropped from rest and falls for 2.5 s. Find the distance it falls and its final speed. [3 marks]
    Show answer

    30.6 m; 24.5 m s⁻¹

    s = ½ × 9.8 × 2.5² = 30.625; v = 9.8 × 2.5.

  2. A car travelling at 20 m s⁻¹ brakes with constant deceleration 2.5 m s⁻². How far does it travel before stopping? [2 marks]
    Show answer

    80 m

    0 = 20² − 2 × 2.5 × s, so s = 400 ÷ 5.

Watch out!

Choose a positive direction and stick to it. If up is positive, then g acts downwards, so a = −9.8, and a point below the start has negative displacement.

Variable acceleration

Year 1 + 2
Notes
  • When acceleration changes with time, use calculus instead of SUVAT.
  • Differentiate to go down the chain: displacement → velocity → acceleration.
  • Integrate to go up the chain, using initial conditions to find the constant.
  • “At rest” means v = 0. Maximum velocity occurs when a = 0.
Key formulas
  • Differentiatev=dsdt,a=dvdt=d2sdt2
  • Integrates=∫vdt,v=∫adt
Worked exampleA particle moves in a straight line with velocity v = 3t² − 12t + 9 m s⁻¹. Find when it is at rest, and its displacement from the start after 1 second.
Show solutionHide solution
  1. At rest: 3t² − 12t + 9 = 3(t − 1)(t − 3) = 0, so t = 1 or t = 3.
  2. Displacement: s = ∫ v dt = t³ − 6t² + 9t (with s = 0 at t = 0).
  3. At t = 1: s = 1 − 6 + 9 = 4.
At rest when t = 1 s and t = 3 s; displacement 4 m
Practice
  1. A particle starts from rest at the origin with acceleration a = 4t m s⁻². Find its velocity and displacement at t = 3. [4 marks]
    Show answer

    v = 18 m s⁻¹, s = 18 m

    v = 2t² (c = 0) and s = 2t³/3 (c = 0).

  2. For the worked example, find the total distance travelled in the first 3 seconds. [3 marks]
    Show answer

    8 m

    It goes 4 m forwards (t = 0 to 1), then turns round. s(3) = 0, so it comes 4 m back. 4 + 4 = 8.

Watch out!

If the particle changes direction during the interval, the integral gives displacement, not distance. Split the integral at the times when v = 0.

Forces & Newton's laws

Year 1 + 2
Notes
  • First law: an object stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.
  • Second law: resultant force = mass × acceleration.
  • Third law: if A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A.
  • For connected particles over a smooth pulley on a light inextensible string, the tension is the same throughout and both particles have the same size of acceleration.
  • Resolve forces into two perpendicular directions (usually horizontal/vertical, or parallel/perpendicular to a slope).
Key formulas
  • Newton's second lawF = ma
  • WeightW = mg
  • Resolving a force F at angle θF cos θ along, F sin θ perpendicular
Worked exampleParticles of mass 5 kg and 3 kg hang either side of a smooth pulley on a light inextensible string. They are released from rest. Find the acceleration and the tension.
Show solutionHide solution
  1. The 5 kg mass moves down and the 3 kg mass moves up, both with acceleration a.
  2. 5 kg: 5g − T = 5a. 3 kg: T − 3g = 3a.
  3. Add: 2g = 8a, so a = 2.45.
  4. Then T = 3(9.8 + 2.45) = 36.75.
a = 2.45 m s⁻², T = 36.75 N (36.8 N to 3 s.f.)
Practice
  1. A lift of mass 600 kg accelerates upwards at 0.5 m s⁻². Find the tension in the cable. [2 marks]
    Show answer

    6180 N

    T − 600g = 600 × 0.5, so T = 5880 + 300.

  2. A car of mass 1200 kg tows a trailer of mass 400 kg. The driving force is 2000 N. Resistances are 300 N on the car and 100 N on the trailer. Find the acceleration and the tension in the tow bar. [4 marks]
    Show answer

    a = 1 m s⁻², T = 500 N

    Whole system: 2000 − 400 = 1600a. Trailer alone: T − 100 = 400 × 1.

Watch out!

Mass (kg) and weight (N) are different. In F = ma, F is the resultant force, so subtract the forces acting against the motion.

Friction & inclined planes

Year 2
Notes
  • Friction opposes motion (or the tendency to move). It can take any value up to its maximum μR.
  • Limiting equilibrium means the object is on the point of moving, so F = μR exactly.
  • On a slope at angle θ, resolve parallel and perpendicular to the slope. Weight has components mg sin θ down the slope and mg cos θ into the slope.
  • The normal reaction R changes if another force pulls at an angle, so always resolve to find it.
Key formulas
  • FrictionF ≤ μR
  • On a slope (no other forces)R = mg cos θdown-slope component mg sin θ
  • Particle about to slip on a slopeμ = tan θ
Worked exampleA 4 kg block is released from rest on a rough slope inclined at 30°. The coefficient of friction is 0.2. Find its acceleration.
Show solutionHide solution
  1. Perpendicular to the slope: R = 4g cos 30° = 33.95 N.
  2. Friction (moving, so limiting): F = 0.2 × 33.95 = 6.79 N, acting up the slope.
  3. Down the slope: 4g sin 30° − 6.79 = 4a, so 19.6 − 6.79 = 4a.
a = 3.20 m s⁻² (3 s.f.)
Practice
  1. A 10 kg box on a rough horizontal floor (μ = 0.3) is pulled by a horizontal force of 50 N. Find its acceleration. [3 marks]
    Show answer

    2.06 m s⁻²

    R = 98, so F = 29.4. Then 50 − 29.4 = 10a.

  2. Find the least coefficient of friction that lets a block rest on a slope at 25° without slipping. [3 marks]
    Show answer

    μ = tan 25° = 0.466

    mg sin 25° ≤ μ mg cos 25°, so μ ≥ tan 25°.

Watch out!

On a slope, R ≠ mg. And if the block is at rest but not about to move, friction is less than μR, so you can't use F = μR.

Projectiles

Year 2
Notes
  • Treat horizontal and vertical motion separately. Time is the only quantity they share.
  • Horizontally: no acceleration (ignoring air resistance), so x = (u cos θ)t.
  • Vertically: SUVAT with a = −g and initial velocity u sin θ.
  • At the highest point the vertical velocity is zero. The speed at any time is √(vx² + vy²).
Key formulas
  • Initial componentsux = u cos θuy = u sin θ
  • Time of flight (level ground)T=2usinθg
  • Maximum heightH=u2sin2θ2g
Worked exampleA ball is projected from level ground at 20 m s⁻¹, 30° above the horizontal. Find the time of flight, the range and the maximum height.
Show solutionHide solution
  1. Components: ux = 20 cos 30° = 17.32, uy = 20 sin 30° = 10.
  2. Lands when sy = 0: 10t − 4.9t² = 0, so t = 10/4.9 = 2.04 s.
  3. Range: 17.32 × 2.041 = 35.3 m.
  4. Max height: 0 = 10² − 2(9.8)s, so s = 5.10 m.
2.04 s, range 35.3 m, max height 5.10 m
Practice
  1. A ball is thrown horizontally at 12 m s⁻¹ from the top of a cliff 40 m high. How far from the foot of the cliff does it land? [3 marks]
    Show answer

    34.3 m

    Vertically: 40 = 4.9t², so t = 2.857 s. Horizontally: 12 × 2.857.

  2. Find the speed of the ball in question 1 as it lands. [3 marks]
    Show answer

    30.5 m s⁻¹

    v_y = 9.8 × 2.857 = 28.0 and v_x = 12, so speed = √(12² + 28²) = √928.

Watch out!

Don't use the total speed in a single SUVAT equation. Split it into components first, and only ever share t between the two directions.

Moments

Year 2
Notes
  • The moment of a force about a point measures its turning effect.
  • A rigid body is in equilibrium when the resultant force is zero and the total moment about every point is zero.
  • The weight of a uniform rod acts at its midpoint.
  • Take moments about a point where an unknown force acts, so it drops out of the equation.
  • When a plank is about to tilt about a support, the reaction at the other support is zero.
Key formulas
  • Momentforce × perpendicular distance from the pivot
  • EquilibriumΣ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments
  • Unitsnewton metres (N m)
Worked exampleA uniform plank AB of length 6 m and mass 20 kg rests on supports at A and B. A child of mass 30 kg stands 2 m from A. Find the reaction at each support.
Show solutionHide solution
  1. The plank's weight acts at its midpoint, 3 m from A.
  2. Moments about A: RB × 6 = 20g × 3 + 30g × 2 = 120g, so RB = 20g.
  3. Resolve vertically: RA + RB = 50g, so RA = 30g.
R_A = 294 N, R_B = 196 N
Practice
  1. A uniform 4 m rod is pivoted at its centre. A 6 kg mass hangs 0.5 m from one end. Where must an 8 kg mass hang on the other side to balance it? [3 marks]
    Show answer

    1.125 m from the centre

    The 6 kg mass is 1.5 m from the centre: 6g × 1.5 = 8g × d.

  2. A uniform plank AB, 6 m long and of mass 20 kg, rests on supports at C (1 m from A) and D (1 m from B). How far beyond D can an 80 kg man stand before the plank tilts? [4 marks]
    Show answer

    0.5 m beyond D

    About to tilt about D, so R_C = 0. Moments about D: 20g × 2 = 80g × x.

Watch out!

Use the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. For a force at an angle, resolve it or use Fd sin θ.
