Pure Mathematics

The core of the qualification — tested in Papers 1 and 2. Strong algebra and calculus skills unlock everything else.

  • Units10
  • Duration30 weeks
  • Exam weight≈ 67%
  • LevelYear 12 & 13
Unit 1

Proof

Proof by deduction, exhaustion, counter-example and contradiction.

Prove √2 is irrational.
Unit 2

Algebra & Functions

Indices, surds, quadratics, inequalities, partial fractions, composite and inverse functions.

f⁻¹(x),   fg(x),   |2x − 3| < 5
Unit 3

Coordinate Geometry

Straight lines, circles and parametric equations.

(x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²
Unit 4

Sequences & Series

Binomial expansion, arithmetic and geometric series, sigma notation.

S∞ = a ⁄ (1 − r),   |r| < 1
Unit 5

Trigonometry

Radians, identities, compound and double angle formulae, R cos(θ ± α).

sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ
Unit 6

Exponentials & Logarithms

Laws of logs, eˣ and ln x, exponential growth and decay models.

loga(xy) = logax + logay
Unit 7

Differentiation

First principles, chain, product and quotient rules, implicit and parametric differentiation.

d/dx [uv] = u v′ + v u′
Unit 8

Integration

Substitution, by parts, partial fractions, areas and differential equations.

∫ u v′ dx = uv − ∫ v u′ dx
Unit 9

Numerical Methods

Locating roots, iteration, Newton–Raphson and the trapezium rule.

xn+1 = xn − f(xn) ⁄ f′(xn)
Unit 10

Vectors

Vectors in 2D and 3D, magnitude, direction and geometric problems.

|a| = √(x² + y² + z²)

Worked example · Integration by parts

Find ∫ x eˣ dx.

  1. Choose u = x and v′ = eˣ.
  2. Then u′ = 1 and v = eˣ.
  3. Apply ∫ u v′ dx = uv − ∫ v u′ dx: x eˣ − ∫ eˣ dx.
∫ x eˣ dx = eˣ(x − 1) + C

Statistics

Half of Paper 3. Learn to model uncertainty, interpret real data and draw valid conclusions.

  • Units6
  • Duration15 weeks
  • Exam weight≈ 17%
  • Large data setIncluded
Unit 1

Sampling

Populations, samples and sampling methods — random, systematic, stratified, quota and opportunity.

Unit 2

Data Presentation

Histograms, box plots, cumulative frequency, outliers, measures of location and spread.

σ² = Σx² ⁄ n − x̄²
Unit 3

Probability

Venn and tree diagrams, mutually exclusive and independent events, conditional probability.

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) ⁄ P(B)
Unit 4

Binomial Distribution

Conditions for modelling, calculating probabilities and cumulative tables.

X ~ B(n, p)
Unit 5

Normal Distribution

Standardising, inverse normal, and approximating the binomial.

Z = (X − μ) ⁄ σ
Unit 6

Hypothesis Testing

One- and two-tailed tests for binomial proportion, normal mean and correlation.

H₀: p = 0.3   H₁: p > 0.3

Worked example · Normal distribution

X ~ N(50, 4²). Find P(X > 56).

  1. Standardise: z = (56 − 50) ⁄ 4 = 1.5.
  2. P(X > 56) = P(Z > 1.5) = 1 − Φ(1.5).
  3. Φ(1.5) = 0.9332 from tables or calculator.
P(X > 56) = 0.0668 (4 d.p.)

Mechanics

The other half of Paper 3. Model motion and forces using vectors, calculus and clear diagrams.

  • Units6
  • Duration15 weeks
  • Exam weight≈ 17%
  • g9.8 m s⁻²
Unit 1

Modelling & Units

Modelling assumptions (particles, light strings, smooth surfaces) and SI units.

Unit 2

Kinematics

Displacement–time and velocity–time graphs, constant acceleration (SUVAT).

v² = u² + 2as
Unit 3

Variable Acceleration

Using calculus when acceleration is a function of time.

v = ds/dt,   a = dv/dt
Unit 4

Forces & Newton's Laws

Resolving forces, connected particles, pulleys and inclined planes.

F = ma
Unit 5

Friction & Projectiles

Coefficient of friction, limiting equilibrium, projectile motion.

F ≤ μR
Unit 6

Moments

Moments about a point, rigid bodies in equilibrium, ladders and beams.

Σ clockwise = Σ anticlockwise

Worked example · SUVAT

A car accelerates uniformly from 4 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹ in 8 s. Find the distance travelled.

  1. Known: u = 4, v = 20, t = 8; want s.
  2. Use s = ½(u + v)t.
  3. s = ½(4 + 20)(8) = 96.
s = 96 m
